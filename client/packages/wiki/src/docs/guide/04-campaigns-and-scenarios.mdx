# Campaigns and scenarios

## Campaigns

A campaign is one group's game in a setting. Its places, people and the rest are its own; the
setting's canon is shared. Each campaign's **About** page has its name, description and origin.

## Scenarios and playthroughs

A **scenario** is a campaign that is never played directly: an adventure, a one-shot, a module. To
play it you **start** it, and the wiki copies it into a new campaign, a **playthrough**:

- every record is copied, with the links between them remapped to the copies;
- inventories are copied, and so are the player characters you pick;
- the playthrough remembers which scenario it came from, and keeps its era.

Starting answers at once and copies in the background, because a big scenario has hundreds of
records. The page follows the copy and opens the playthrough when it's done. A start that fails is
undone, so a half-copied campaign never stays behind.

The scenario panel on a scenario's page starts it. A start can also bring along the setting's
**pregens**, ready-made characters tagged *Pregen* (`GET /v1/setting/<id>/pregen` lists them, and
the start request names the ones to copy).

## Framing

A scenario's framing is a journal of kind `campaign_framing`: its premise, opening situation,
player briefing and starting location. The AI game master reads it to open the game.

## Origin

Every campaign and record says where it came from:

- **Homebrew**: yours;
- **Official**: from a published book or system, with its source;
- **Adapted**: changed from one, with its source.

Homebrew has no source. The origin panel on each page shows and edits it.

## Play styles

Scenarios can be tagged with how they play: freeform, combat heavy, narrative, mystery and so on.
These are shared tags every instance starts with; you can add your own beside them.

## Elevating a person

A person made in a playthrough (an NPC the game master improvised, say) can be copied back into its
scenario with **Elevate to scenario**, so the next playthrough has them too. Their place, ties and
tags are matched by name.
